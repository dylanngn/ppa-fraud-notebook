\section{Exploratory Data Analysis}
\label{sec:3_3}

Exploratory analysis serves as the foundation for pattern discovery, revealing fraud signatures in temporal, geographic, and entity-relationship dimensions before any modeling commences.

\textbf{Critical Distinction:} The event-level fraud rate (1.21\%) is misleading. Legitimate listings generate more events on average (9.7 events) than fraudulent listings (4.8 events) because they progress through the full lifecycle (creation $\rightarrow$ publication $\rightarrow$ archival) while fraudulent listings are terminated early. All subsequent analysis operates at listing level where the fraud rate is 8.11\%, representing moderate class imbalance (approximately 1:11 ratio).

\subsection{Fraud Pattern Discovery}

\textbf{Temporal Patterns:} Analysis of fraud distribution across time dimensions revealed several signatures:

Hourly distribution shows fraud activity peaks between 6-7 AM local time, suggesting automated submission or activity from different time zones. Legitimate listings show more uniform distribution across business hours.

Monthly analysis revealed a significant spike in February 2025, with fraud rates reaching 13.12\%-62\% above the annual average. Rates subsequently declined to approximately 5\% by mid-2025, potentially indicating successful intervention or fraudster adaptation.

Day-of-week analysis shows elevated fraud rates on weekends (Saturday: 9.2\%, Sunday: 8.8\%) compared to weekdays (7.4\% average), consistent with reduced human oversight during off-hours.

Studio rentals represent the highest-risk category, likely because lower price points attract more victims and require less sophisticated property documentation. The stark differential between rental (9.47\%) and purchase (0.88\%) fraud rates reflects the lower verification requirements for rental listings.

\subsection{Entity-Sharing Analysis}

Analysis of device fingerprints, IP addresses, and identity markers reveals network structure potentially encoding fraud rings.

\textbf{Device Sharing Patterns:}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Device sharing statistics.}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\hline
Metric & Value \\
\hline
Total unique devices & 16,447 \\
Devices with 2+ listings & 6,473 (39.4\%) \\
Multi-listing devices with any fraud & 1,322 (20.4\%) \\
Super-connectors (10+ listings) & 1,248 \\
Super-connector fraud rate & 35.5\% \\
Maximum listings per device & 6,251 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The maximum of 6,251 listings from a single device fingerprint represents an unmistakable fraud ring signature no legitimate user creates thousands of listings. Super-connector devices (10+ listings) show 35.5\% fraud rate compared to 8.11\% baseline, a 4.4$\times$ lift indicating strong predictive signal.

\textbf{Cross-Platform Activity:} Listings appearing on both Homegate and ImmoScout24 show 26.74\% fraud rate (3.3$\times$ lift). While legitimate property managers may cross-post, the combination with other risk indicators (new accounts, suspicious infrastructure) amplifies fraud signal.

\textbf{IP Address Clustering:} IP addresses show similar patterns with some noise from shared corporate/residential networks. The top IP addresses connect to hundreds of listings, though interpretation requires caution as legitimate corporate users may share IP infrastructure.

These entity-sharing patterns motivate the graph construction methodology in Section 3.5, where device/IP/email/phone relationships form edges connecting listings into a heterogeneous fraud detection graph.
