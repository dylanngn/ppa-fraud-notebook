Our findings reveal several limitations and open questions that position future research directions. We organize these by timeline and compare with how prior work has addressed similar challenges.

\textbf{Short-Term Extensions:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{MLOps pipeline with automated retraining:} Our temporal validation revealed 70\% GNN performance degradation under concept drift. While prior work on financial fraud \cite{gama2014survey} addresses drift through periodic retraining, our finding that drift operates at the \emph{topology level} (new fraud campaigns form disconnected components) suggests graph-aware drift detection beyond DDM/ADWIN is needed.
    \item \textbf{Shadow deployment:} Direct comparison against SEON in production would resolve the limitation (Section~\ref{sec:5_10_2}, Limitation 10) that our SEON baseline uses aggregated scores rather than identical evaluation conditions.
    \item \textbf{Threshold optimization:} Current results use threshold 0.5; business-aware calibration based on investigation capacity and fraud loss functions could improve operational utility, following the cost-sensitive learning approaches of \cite{elkan2001foundations}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Medium-Term Research:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Real-time GNN inference:} Our current approach requires batch graph construction. Incremental GNN methods \cite{zhang2022streaming} could enable streaming predictions, addressing the latency gap between tabular scoring (sub-ms) and graph embedding generation ($\sim$100ms).
    \item \textbf{Cross-platform federation:} Fraud rings operate across Homegate and ImmoScout24 (26.74\% cross-platform fraud rate). Federated learning approaches \cite{yang2019federated} could share fraud signals without centralizing sensitive data, extending the graph beyond single-platform boundaries.
    \item \textbf{Active learning:} Our ground truth depends on existing detection systems (Limitation 3). Incorporating fraud analyst feedback through active learning \cite{settles2009active} could improve label quality and detect sophisticated fraud that evades current methods.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Long-Term Directions:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Temporal GNNs:} Our static graph collapses temporal ordering (Limitation 5). Temporal Graph Networks (TGN) \cite{rossi2020temporal} and Temporal Graph Attention (TGAT) \cite{xu2020inductive} capture time-evolving patterns, potentially resolving the temporal instability that limits GNN production deployment. Prior work on financial transactions shows 5-15\% improvement from temporal modeling \cite{pareja2020evolvegcn}.
    \item \textbf{Transfer learning:} Our results derive from Swiss real estate (Limitation 4). Transfer learning across European marketplace platforms would test generalization, comparing against domain adaptation approaches in fraud detection \cite{cao2019transfer}.
    \item \textbf{Adversarial robustness:} Sophisticated fraudsters may adapt to learned patterns. Adversarial GNN training \cite{zugner2018adversarial} could improve robustness against evasion attacks, an unexplored dimension in real estate fraud research.
\end{itemize}
