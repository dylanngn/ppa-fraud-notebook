Our findings have identified several open questions and technical challenges that point toward promising future research directions. We have organized these opportunities by their implementation timeline and compared them with existing literature.

\paragraph{Short-Term Extensions}
In the immediate future, several improvements could enhance the operational utility of the current system. First, developing a more robust MLOps pipeline with automated retraining is essential. Our research showed a significant performance drop for GNNs under concept drift, and while standard methods like DDM/ADWIN can flag these shifts, we need more "graph-aware" detection that can account for changes in network topology. Second, a formal shadow deployment would allow for a direct, head-to-head comparison between our models and the existing SEON system under identical production conditions. Finally, optimizing decision thresholds based on actual business costs and investigation capacityrather than just the default 0.5would further improve the practical value of the model.

\paragraph{Medium-Term Research}
Over the medium term, we see opportunities for more fundamental shifts in the system's architecture. Transitioning to real-time GNN inference would close the current gap between tabular scoring, which is nearly instantaneous, and graph embedding generation, which currently takes significantly longer. We also believe that cross-platform federation between Homegate and ImmoScout24 could be highly beneficial. Given the high rate of cross-platform fraud, a federated learning approach would allow both platforms to benefit from shared signals without compromising sensitive user data. Furthermore, incorporating active learning could help refine our ground truth labels by bringing fraud analysts into the loop, allowing the model to learn from human expertise on the most ambiguous cases.

\paragraph{Long-Term Directions}
In the long term, moving toward more complex modeling architectures could resolve some of the fundamental stability issues we identified. Temporal Graph Networks (TGNs) and similar time-aware architectures could capture how relationships evolve, potentially fixing the "temporal brittleness" that currently limits the production use of GNNs. We also see a need for broader validation of these methods through transfer learning. Testing these models on other European marketplaces would determine if the patterns we found in Swiss real estate are universal or specific to this market. Finally, exploring the adversarial robustness of these models will be critical as fraudsters become more aware of graph-based detection and begin to adapt their network structures to evade it.
